   carbon.txt: A transparency protocol for public sustainability data
                    draft-cowlishaw-carbontxt-latest

Abstract

   This document specifies the carbon.txt convention, a machine-readable
   format for publishing links to an origin's sustainability disclosures
   at a predictable location on the web.  Carbon.txt is a TOML document
   that acts as an index: it declares where an origin's sustainability
   information lives (Annual reports, Sustainability policies, Third
   party certifications, Quantitative measurements or estimations) and
   optionally lists upstream service providers whose own disclosures may
   be relevant to the origin's environmental impact.  Carbon.txt does
   not carry numeric impact metrics or specific sustainability claims
   itself; it links to documents that do.  In addition it defines
   discovery mechanism by which certain origins may delegate their
   sustainability disclosures to another.

1.  Introduction

   Organisations increasingly publish sustainability information
   relevant to their digital operations — Impact reports, energy mix
   disclosures and guarantees of origin, regulatory filings under
   frameworks such as the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting
   Directive (CSRD), and measurements of CO2 impact; but this
   information is scattered across PDFs, web pages, and third-party
   registries with no standard way for a client to discover it
   programmatically.

   The carbon.txt convention addresses this by providing a single,
   predictable location on an origin where a machine-readable index of
   those disclosures can be found.  It is analogous to robots.txt for
   crawler instructions or security.txt for security contact
   information: a small file at a well-known path that tells a client
   where to look for structured information about the origin.

1.1.  Design principles:

   *  carbon.txt is an index, not a data store.  It links to documents;
      it does not embed specific claims or measurements.

   *  It is human-readable (TOML with comments) as well as machine-
      parsable.

   *  It is designed for incremental deployment.  A minimal valid file
      can be as simple as a link to a single disclosure.

   *  It does not in itself verify or guarantee the veracity of claims
      or disclosures, but provides mechanisms for third-party
      validation.

1.2.  Relationship to other work

   This document is complementary to the sustainability-data well-known
   URI proposal made by [I-D.besleaga-sustainability-wellknown].  The
   sustainability-data draft outlines a mechanism for declaring the
   energy consumption, carbon footprint, and related environmental and
   sustainability metrics of an origin for different reporting periods,
   while this proposal explicitly considers the sharing of such metrics
   to be out of scope, concentrating instead on providing a mechanism
   for indexing sustainability disclosures (such we a sustainability-
   data declaration) related to an origin at a predictable location.
   The two proposals compose cleanly: An implementation of carbon.txt
   may link to their sustainability-data as a disclosure, and a
   sustainability-data file may link back to carbon-txt via the
   disclosure-uri field.

2.  Conventions and Definitions

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

   Origin: As defined in [RFC6454].  A tuple of scheme, host, and port.

   Disclosure: A published document or resource that contains
   sustainability-related information about an organisation.

   Upstream service: A third-party provider whose services are used by
   the origin and whose sustainability disclosures may be relevant to
   the origin's environmental impact (e.g., hosting, CDN, analytics).

   carbon.txt file: A TOML document conforming to the schema defined in
   Section 5 of this specification.
